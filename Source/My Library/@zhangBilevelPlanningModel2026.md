# A bi-level planning model of integrated energy capacity configuration for data center cluster considering computing-electricity coupling operation and supply-demand uncertainties
## Abstract
> With the rapid expansion of data centers, their energy consumption has also increased substantially. Renewable-integrated energy systems are emerging as a promising paradigm for data center clusters. Through coordinated computing and power dispatch, they effectively address the spatiotemporal mismatch between energy supply and demand, thereby supporting the green and low-carbon development of new-type power system of China. However, supply-demand uncertainties caused by renewable generation and computing workloads increase planning complexity and may result in resource redundancy and operational imbalance. This study develops a two-stage distributionally robust bi-level capacity configuration model for data center cluster that jointly optimizes capacity configuration and computing-electricity coordinated operation. The model characterizes temporal shifting and spatial migration of workloads and establishes a coupling mechanism among computing demand, electricity consumption, cooling demand, and energy supply. To address supply-demand uncertainties, a joint moment-based ambiguity set is constructed to capture the statistical dependence between renewable generation and computing workloads. The upper level minimizes the lifecycle equivalent annual cost for capacity planning, while the lower level minimizes operating cost and net load deviation under the worst-case distributions. Case studies show that incorporating workload flexibility increases renewable energy utilization by 1.18% and reduces total system cost by 5.43% while enhancing planning robustness. Compared with conventional planning methods, the proposed model achieves a more balanced allocation of renewable generation, energy storage, and cooling capacity. The proposed framework improves economic performance, operational reliability, and renewable energy accommodation, providing an effective solution for computing-electricity coordinated planning of low-carbon data center cluster. © 2026 Elsevier Ltd
